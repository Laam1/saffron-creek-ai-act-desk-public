# TB-01: Loan Approver

## Risk tier

high-risk

## Why

Quote check: on 8 November 2026 every quote from S-01 below was compared word for word with the official EUR-Lex text, and each one matched. S-02 was read only to see whether it changes these quotes. It does not (Art. 1 points 2, 3 and 8, S-02). The other source texts were not opened. See "What's missing".

**Step 1: Scope. The system is in scope.**
- The register says the output is used in the UAE and France. France is in the EU.
- The bank is licensed in the DIFC, which is outside the EU. The AI Act reaches "providers and deployers of AI systems that have their place of establishment or are located in a third country, where the output produced by the AI system is used in the Union" (Art. 2(1)(c), S-01).
- The system was built in-house and the bank also uses it. So the bank looks like both provider and deployer (Art. 3 points 3 and 4, S-01).

**Step 2: Article 5 bans. No ban matched on the register's facts.**
- The register describes scoring of personal loan applications. Nothing in it points to a banned practice (Art. 5, S-01).
- This result is limited. The register does not list the data the model uses. See "What's missing".

**Step 3: Annex III. Matched.**
- The system scores personal loan applications from retail customers, and it approves or declines loans.
- This matches Annex III point 5(b): "AI systems intended to be used to evaluate the creditworthiness of natural persons or establish their credit score, with the exception of AI systems used for the purpose of detecting financial fraud" (Annex III point 5(b), S-01).
- The register shows no fraud-detection purpose, so that exception does not appear to apply (Annex III point 5(b), S-01).
- An Annex III system is high-risk under Article 6(2) (Art. 6(2), S-01).
- The Article 6(3) way out does not appear to be available. The system decides the outcome of the loan, so its influence is material (Art. 6(3), S-01).
- Scoring individual applicants also looks like profiling. Article 6(3) says: "Notwithstanding the first subparagraph, an AI system referred to in Annex III shall always be considered to be high-risk where the AI system performs profiling of natural persons" (Art. 6(3), S-01). The register does not use the word profiling, so a human should confirm this (Art. 3 point 52, S-01).

**Step 4: Article 50. Not settled, and it does not change the tier.**
- The register does not say whether customers interact directly with the system (Art. 50, S-01).

**Step 5: Minimal. Does not apply.**
- The system matched Annex III at step 3 (Annex III point 5(b), S-01).

**What being high-risk brings**
- Requirements for high-risk systems are in Articles 8 to 15 (Arts. 8 to 15, S-01).
- Duties of providers and deployers are in Articles 16 to 27 (Arts. 16 to 27, S-01).
- Some of these were amended by the Digital Omnibus (Art. 1 points 9 to 13, S-02). The amended wording was not read.

## DIFC notes

- The bank is licensed in the DIFC, according to `bank/profile.md`. Whether the DIFC Data Protection Law applies to this system turns on its application article (Art. 6, S-04). That article was not read, so this is unknown.
- TB-01 uses personal data and approves or declines loans. Regulation 10 covers processing personal data through autonomous and semi-autonomous systems, so it looks relevant (Regulation 10, S-03). Which duties in sections 10.1 to 10.3 apply is unknown, because the text was not read.
- These DIFC points need checking, because they cover this kind of system: automated individual decision-making including Profiling (Art. 38, S-04), data protection impact assessment (Art. 20, S-04), and the High Risk Processing Activities definition (Schedule 1, S-04).
- No DIFC gap has been found or ruled out. The review could not be done without the text.
