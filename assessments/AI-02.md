# AI-02: Website Chat Assistant

## Risk tier
needs lawyer

## Why
- The first step is scope. Scope means whether the law applies to the system at all.
- The EU AI Act covers providers (makers) and deployers (users) based outside the EU "where the output produced by the AI system is used in the Union" (Art. 2(1)(c), S-01). An "output" is a result the system produces, here an answer to a customer.
- The register says where this output is used is unknown. So we cannot tell whether the Act applies.
- The desk rules say to stop here. The checks against Article 5, Annex III and Article 50 were not run.

## Deadline
unknown. The deadline depends on the tier, and the tier cannot be set until scope is known (Art. 2(1)(c), S-01).

## DIFC notes
DIFC Regulation 10 covers personal data processed by AI (Reg. 10.1.1(a), S-03). The EU question above does not settle this section. These are duties to check. We hold no evidence yet on whether they are met.
- AI-02 uses personal data (register). It is a "System" if it works "in an autonomous or semi-autonomous manner" and produces output from that data (Reg. 10.1.1(a), S-03).
- The bank is the "Deployer", meaning the person for whose benefit the system runs, even if someone else hosts it (Reg. 10.1.1(b), S-03). A Deployer is treated as a Controller, meaning the party responsible for the personal data (Reg. 10.3.4(a), S-03).
- AI-02 is a website service. So notice must be given "in clear and explicit terms upon the initial use of, or access to, the System" (Reg. 10.2.2(a), S-03). The notice must describe the purposes and the output (Reg. 10.2.2(b), S-03).
- The system must be designed to be ethical, fair, transparent, secure and accountable (Reg. 10.3.1, S-03).
- The DIFC Data Protection Law applies to processing by a Controller "incorporated in the DIFC", or processing in the DIFC "as part of stable arrangements" (Art. 6(3), S-04). The bank is licensed in the DIFC (`bank/profile.md`). Whether it is incorporated there: unknown.
- Personal data must be processed "lawfully, fairly and in a transparent manner" and for specified purposes (Art. 9(1)(b) and (c), S-04). The bank needs a lawful basis, meaning a legal reason to use the data (Art. 10, S-04).
- The register says AI-02 gives information only. On that fact, the right to object to decisions "based solely on automated Processing" does not appear to be triggered (Art. 38(1), S-04).

## What's missing

- Whether EU data protection law (the GDPR) applies to AI-02: unknown. It can reach a bank outside the EU whose processing is related to "the offering of goods or services" to people in the EU (Art. 3(2)(a), S-05). The bank has customers in Germany, so this needs checking even if the AI output turns out not to be used in the EU.
- Whether the bank is incorporated in the DIFC (Art. 6(3), S-04), and its lawful basis for AI-02 (Art. 10, S-04): unknown.
- Whether AI-02 counts as "High Risk Processing Activities" (Schedule 1, S-04): unknown. This is the DIFC term for uses of personal data that bring extra duties, such as an impact assessment (Art. 20(1), S-04). It includes a "systematic and extensive evaluation of personal aspects" based on automated processing, where decisions based on it significantly affect people (Schedule 1, S-04).
- Whether S-04 is the latest version of the DIFC law: unknown. It is dated July 2025, and the DIFC status pages could not be opened.
- Whether AI-02 works autonomously or semi-autonomously (Reg. 10.1.1(a), S-03): unknown.
- The vendor's role, and whether it runs the system for the bank as an "Operator" (Reg. 10.1.1(c), S-03): unknown.
