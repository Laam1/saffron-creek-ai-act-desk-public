# AI-06: Offer Personaliser

## Risk tier
needs lawyer

## Why
- The first step is scope. Scope means whether the law applies to the system at all.
- The EU AI Act covers providers (makers) and deployers (users) based outside the EU "where the output produced by the AI system is used in the Union" (Art. 2(1)(c), S-01). An "output" is a result the system produces, here the choice of which offers a customer sees.
- The register says where this output is used is unknown. So we cannot tell whether the Act applies.
- The desk rules say to stop here. The checks against Article 5, Annex III and Article 50 were not run.

## Deadline
unknown. The deadline depends on the tier, and the tier cannot be set until scope is known (Art. 2(1)(c), S-01).

## DIFC notes
DIFC Regulation 10 covers personal data processed by AI. The EU question above does not settle this section. These are duties to check. We hold no evidence yet on whether they are met.
- AI-06 uses personal data (register). It is a "System" if it works "in an autonomous or semi-autonomous manner" and produces output from that data (Reg. 10.1.1(a), S-03).
- The bank is the "Deployer", meaning the person for whose benefit the system runs (Reg. 10.1.1(b), S-03). A Deployer is treated as a Controller, meaning the party responsible for the personal data (Reg. 10.3.4(a), S-03).
- The system must be designed to be ethical, fair, transparent, secure and accountable (Reg. 10.3.1, S-03).
- It may only process personal data for human-defined or human-approved purposes, or within human-set limits (Reg. 10.3.2(a), S-03).
- Where an application or website is used, the bank must give clear notice at first use (Reg. 10.2.2(a) and (b), S-03).
- Customers may complain to challenge the outcome (Reg. 10.3.5, S-03).
- The DIFC Data Protection Law applies to processing by a Controller "incorporated in the DIFC", or processing in the DIFC "as part of stable arrangements" (Art. 6(3), S-04). The bank is licensed in the DIFC (`bank/profile.md`). Whether it is incorporated there: unknown.
- Personal data must be processed "lawfully, fairly and in a transparent manner" and for specified purposes (Art. 9(1)(b) and (c), S-04). The bank needs a lawful basis, meaning a legal reason to use the data (Art. 10, S-04).
- If the offers are direct marketing, customers may "object at any time to such Processing, including Profiling to the extent that it is related to such direct marketing" (Art. 34(1)(c), S-04). Profiling means using automated processing to evaluate a person.
