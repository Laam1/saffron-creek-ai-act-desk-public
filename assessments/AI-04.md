# AI-04: CV Screener

## Risk tier
needs lawyer

## Why
- The first step is scope. Scope means whether the law applies to the system at all.
- The EU AI Act covers providers (makers) and deployers (users) based outside the EU "where the output produced by the AI system is used in the Union" (Art. 2(1)(c), S-01). An "output" is a result the system produces, here a ranking of job applicants.
- The register says where this output is used is unknown. So we cannot tell whether the Act applies.
- The desk rules say to stop here. The checks against Article 5, Annex III and Article 50 were not run.

## Deadline
unknown. The deadline depends on the tier, and the tier cannot be set until scope is known (Art. 2(1)(c), S-01).

## DIFC notes
DIFC Regulation 10 covers personal data processed by AI. The EU question above does not settle this section. These are duties to check. We hold no evidence yet on whether they are met.
- AI-04 uses personal data (register). It is a "System" if it works "in an autonomous or semi-autonomous manner" and produces output from that data (Reg. 10.1.1(a), S-03).
- The bank is the "Deployer", meaning the person for whose benefit the system runs, even if someone else hosts it (Reg. 10.1.1(b), S-03). A Deployer is treated as a Controller, meaning the party responsible for the personal data (Reg. 10.3.4(a), S-03).
- The system must be designed to treat people "equally and fairly, regardless of race, gender, or other specifically subjective factors" (Reg. 10.3.1(b), S-03).
- Where an application or website is used, the bank must give clear notice at first use (Reg. 10.2.2(a), S-03). On request it must show evidence of how the system seeks human help when the result may be unfair or discriminatory, plus a risk and impact assessment (Reg. 10.2.2(d), S-03).
- Applicants may complain to challenge the outcome (Reg. 10.3.5, S-03).
- The DIFC Data Protection Law applies to processing by a Controller "incorporated in the DIFC", or processing in the DIFC "as part of stable arrangements" (Art. 6(3), S-04). The bank is licensed in the DIFC (`bank/profile.md`). Whether it is incorporated there: unknown.
- Personal data must be processed "lawfully, fairly and in a transparent manner" and for specified purposes (Art. 9(1)(b) and (c), S-04). The bank needs a lawful basis, meaning a legal reason to use the data (Art. 10, S-04).
- The register says AI-04 "decides who is invited to interview". If no person reviews that, applicants may "object to any decision based solely on automated Processing" that has legal or seriously impactful consequences, and require manual review (Art. 38(1), S-04).
- CVs may reveal "Special Categories of Personal Data", such as health or religious beliefs (Schedule 1, S-04). These need extra grounds (Art. 11, S-04). Decisions based solely on automated use of such data are restricted (Art. 38(6), S-04).
- AI-04 may be "High Risk Processing Activities", which include a "systematic and extensive evaluation of personal aspects" based on automated processing, where decisions based on it significantly affect people (Schedule 1, S-04). If so, an impact assessment is needed first (Art. 20(1), S-04), a data protection officer may be needed (Art. 16(2)(b), S-04), and extra conditions apply (Reg. 10.3.3, S-03).
