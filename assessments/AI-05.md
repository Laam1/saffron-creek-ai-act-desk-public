# AI-05: Call Emotion Scorer

## Risk tier
needs lawyer

## Why
- The first step is scope. Scope means whether the law applies to the system at all.
- The EU AI Act covers providers (makers) and deployers (users) based outside the EU "where the output produced by the AI system is used in the Union" (Art. 2(1)(c), S-01). An "output" is a result the system produces, here an emotion score that feeds staff performance reviews.
- The register says where this output is used is unknown. So we cannot tell whether the Act applies.
- The desk rules say to stop here. The checks against Article 5, Annex III and Article 50 were not run.

## Deadline
unknown. The deadline depends on the tier, and the tier cannot be set until scope is known (Art. 2(1)(c), S-01).

## DIFC notes
DIFC Regulation 10 covers personal data processed by AI (Reg. 10.1.1(a), S-03). The EU question above does not settle this section. These are duties to check. We hold no evidence yet on whether they are met.
- AI-05 uses personal data (register). It is a "System" if it works "in an autonomous or semi-autonomous manner" and produces output from that data (Reg. 10.1.1(a), S-03).
- The bank is the "Deployer", meaning the person for whose benefit the system runs, even if someone else hosts it (Reg. 10.1.1(b), S-03). A Deployer is treated as a Controller, meaning the party responsible for the personal data (Reg. 10.3.4(a), S-03).
- The system must be designed to be ethical, fair, transparent, secure and accountable (Reg. 10.3.1, S-03). Transparent means explainable to people "in non-technical terms" (Reg. 10.3.1(c), S-03).
- Staff may complain to challenge the outcome (Reg. 10.3.5, S-03).
- The DIFC Data Protection Law applies to processing by a Controller "incorporated in the DIFC", or processing in the DIFC "as part of stable arrangements" (Art. 6(3), S-04). The bank is licensed in the DIFC (`bank/profile.md`). Whether it is incorporated there: unknown.
- Personal data must be processed "lawfully, fairly and in a transparent manner" and for specified purposes (Art. 9(1)(b) and (c), S-04). The bank needs a lawful basis, meaning a legal reason to use the data (Art. 10, S-04).
- If no person reviews the result, staff may "object to any decision based solely on automated Processing" that has legal or seriously impactful consequences, and require manual review (Art. 38(1), S-04).
- AI-05 may be "High Risk Processing Activities". The definition expressly counts "staff and contractor Personal Data" and also covers a "systematic and extensive evaluation of personal aspects" (Schedule 1, S-04).
- If so, an impact assessment is needed first (Art. 20(1), S-04), and a data protection officer may be needed (Art. 16(2)(b), S-04). Extra conditions also apply, including audit and certification requirements and an Autonomous Systems Officer, a role like a data protection officer (Reg. 10.3.3, S-03). The Commissioner is the DIFC's data protection regulator. The guidance says "no System may be used for High Risk Processing Activities until the Commissioner has promulgated these certification and other requirements" (Guidance on Reg. 10.3.3, S-03).
- "Special Categories of Personal Data" need extra grounds (Art. 11, S-04). The list includes health data and biometric data "used for the purpose of uniquely identifying a natural person" (Schedule 1, S-04). It does not name emotion data.

- **If the output is used in the EU: possible ban.** This flag does not decide the tier, and the full checks have not been run. Article 5 bans AI systems "to infer emotions of a natural person in the areas of workplace and education institutions", "except where the use of the AI system is intended to be put in place or into the market for medical or safety reasons" (Art. 5(1)(f), S-01). The register says AI-05 "Scores staff members' emotions during calls". Whether a medical or safety reason applies: unknown. Article 5 has applied since 2 February 2025 (Art. 113, third paragraph, point (a), as replaced by Art. 1 point 40(a), S-02).
- Whether EU data protection law (the GDPR) applies to AI-05: unknown. Its reach depends on whether the bank has an establishment in the EU or processes data of people in the EU (Art. 3, S-05). A lawyer must confirm how that works for staff.
