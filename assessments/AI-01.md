# AI-01: Credit Scorer

## Risk tier
high-risk

## Why
**Step 1: Scope. The EU AI Act applies.** Scope means whether the law applies to the system at all.
- The register says the output is used in the UAE and Germany. Germany is in the EU. An "output" is a result the system produces, here a loan decision.
- The Act covers providers (makers) and deployers (users) based outside the EU "where the output produced by the AI system is used in the Union" (Art. 2(1)(c), S-01).

**Step 2: Article 5 bans. No match on the facts we hold, but this check is not finished.**
- Article 5 lists banned AI practices. The closest is social scoring. That ban needs a score that leads to bad treatment in "social contexts that are unrelated to the contexts in which the data was originally generated or collected", or treatment that is "unjustified or disproportionate" (Art. 5(1)(c), S-01).
- The register shows a loan score used for a loan decision. It does not show either result. The input data is unknown, so see "What's missing". If that data shows a match, the tier changes.

**Step 3: Annex III. Match found.**
- Annex III is the list of high-risk AI uses. It includes "AI systems intended to be used to evaluate the creditworthiness of natural persons or establish their credit score, with the exception of AI systems used for the purpose of detecting financial fraud" (Annex III, point 5(b), S-01).
- AI-01 scores personal loan applications and "approves or declines loans" (register). Its purpose is credit scoring, not fraud detection.
- Systems listed in Annex III are high-risk (Art. 6(2), S-01).
- There is an exception for a system that poses no significant risk, "including by not materially influencing the outcome of decision making" (Art. 6(3), S-01). AI-01 does not appear to fit it. An Annex III system "shall always be considered to be high-risk" where it "performs profiling of natural persons" (Art. 6(3), last subparagraph, S-01). Profiling means automated use of personal data to evaluate a person, for example their "economic situation" (Art. 3(52), S-01, and Art. 4(4), S-05). AI-01 scores loan applicants, so it appears to do this. The register also says it approves or declines loans.

**Step 4: Article 50. Open, but it does not change the tier.**
- Article 50 sets duties to tell people about AI. One duty covers systems "intended to interact directly with natural persons" (Art. 50(1), S-01). The register does not say whether applicants interact with AI-01 directly.
- Article 50 duties sit alongside the high-risk duties (Art. 50(6), S-01).

**Step 5: Minimal.** Not available, because AI-01 is high-risk (Art. 6(2), S-01).

**Alongside the tier: EU data protection law. This does not change the tier.**
- The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) covers a controller outside the EU when its processing is related to "the offering of goods or services" to people in the EU (Art. 3(2)(a), S-05). A controller is the party responsible for the personal data. The bank sells loans to customers in Germany (`bank/profile.md`), so the GDPR appears to apply.
- People have "the right not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing" that has legal or similarly significant effects (Art. 22(1), S-05). There are exceptions (Art. 22(2), S-05). Some of them require "at least the right to obtain human intervention" (Art. 22(3), S-05).
- A data protection impact assessment is required for "a systematic and extensive evaluation of personal aspects relating to natural persons" based on automated processing, where decisions based on it have legal or similarly significant effects (Art. 35(3)(a), S-05). This is a written check of the risks to people's data.

## Deadline
- **2 December 2027.** The high-risk rules in Chapter III, Sections 1, 2 and 3 apply from this date to Annex III systems (Art. 113, third paragraph, point (c)(i), as replaced by Art. 1 point 40(b), S-02).
- The law that set this date entered into force on the third day after publication (Art. 4, S-02). It was published on 24 July 2026, so that is 27 July 2026.
- **Possible grace rule.** A high-risk system already in service before that date is covered only if, "as from that date, those systems are subject to significant changes in their designs" (Art. 111(2), as replaced by Art. 1 point 39(a), S-02). The date AI-01 went into service is unknown. Do not rely on this without a lawyer.
- If the Article 50(1) duty applies, its date has already passed. Article 50 falls under the general date of 2 August 2026 (Art. 113, second paragraph, S-01).

**What must be ready by 2 December 2027.** The list depends on the bank's role.
- **Role.** A provider develops an AI system and "puts the AI system into service under its own name or trademark" (Art. 3(3), S-01). A deployer is someone "using an AI system under its authority" (Art. 3(4), S-01). The bank built AI-01 and uses it, so it appears to be both. A lawyer must confirm this, because "putting into service" refers to "own use in the Union" (Art. 3(11), S-01) and the bank is outside the EU.
- **If provider: the system.** Risk management (Art. 9, S-01), data governance (Art. 10, S-01, as amended by Art. 1 point 9, S-02), technical documentation (Art. 11, S-01, as amended by Art. 1 point 10, S-02), record-keeping (Art. 12, S-01), information for deployers (Art. 13, S-01), human oversight (Art. 14, S-01), and accuracy, robustness and cybersecurity (Art. 15, S-01).
- **If provider: the organisation.** A quality management system (Art. 17, S-01, with paragraph 2 replaced by Art. 1 point 11, S-02), kept documents and logs, a conformity assessment (a check that the system meets the rules), an EU declaration of conformity, CE marking (the EU conformity mark) and registration (Art. 16(c) to (i), S-01).
- **If provider outside the EU.** An authorised representative, meaning an agent based in the EU, is needed "Prior to making their high-risk AI systems available on the Union market" (Art. 22(1), S-01). Whether in-house use counts: unknown.
- **If deployer.** Use the system according to its instructions (Art. 26(1), S-01). Assign human oversight to competent people (Art. 26(2), S-01). Check input data where the bank controls it (Art. 26(4), S-01). Monitor the system (Art. 26(5), S-01). Keep logs for at least six months (Art. 26(6), S-01). Tell people that they are subject to the system (Art. 26(11), S-01).
- **Fundamental rights impact assessment.** This is required before use by "deployers of high-risk AI systems referred to in points 5 (b) and (c) of Annex III" (Art. 27(1), S-01). AI-01 falls under point 5(b). S-02 replaces paragraphs 4 and 5 of Art. 27, which now let a deployer reuse parts of a data protection impact assessment, and does not change these words (Art. 1 point 13, S-02).

## DIFC notes
DIFC Regulation 10 covers personal data processed by AI (Reg. 10.1.1(a), S-03). These are duties to check. We hold no evidence yet on whether they are met.
- AI-01 uses personal data (register). It is a "System" if it works "in an autonomous or semi-autonomous manner" and produces output from that data (Reg. 10.1.1(a), S-03).
- The bank is the "Deployer", meaning the person for whose benefit the system runs (Reg. 10.1.1(b), S-03). A Deployer is treated as a Controller, meaning the party responsible for the personal data (Reg. 10.3.4(a), S-03).
- The system must be designed to be ethical, fair, transparent, secure and accountable (Reg. 10.3.1, S-03). Transparent means explainable to people "in non-technical terms" (Reg. 10.3.1(c), S-03).
- It may only process personal data for human-defined or human-approved purposes, or within human-set limits (Reg. 10.3.2(a), S-03).
- Where an application or website is used, the bank must give clear notice at first use (Reg. 10.2.2(a) and (b), S-03). On request it must show evidence of how the system seeks human help when the result may be unfair or discriminatory, plus a risk and impact assessment (Reg. 10.2.2(d), S-03).
- In that case, on request it must also give a register that says whether the system is used "solely to make automated decisions" (Reg. 10.2.2(g)(iii), S-03).
- Customers may complain to challenge the outcome (Reg. 10.3.5, S-03).
- The DIFC Data Protection Law applies to processing by a Controller "incorporated in the DIFC", or processing in the DIFC "as part of stable arrangements" (Art. 6(3), S-04). The bank is licensed in the DIFC (`bank/profile.md`). Whether it is incorporated there: unknown.
- Personal data must be processed "lawfully, fairly and in a transparent manner" and for specified purposes (Art. 9(1)(b) and (c), S-04). The bank needs a lawful basis, meaning a legal reason to use the data, such as a contract with the customer (Art. 10(b), S-04).
- Where needed for fair processing, customers must be told about automated decision-making, with "meaningful information about the logic involved" (Art. 29(1)(h)(vi), S-04).
- A person may "object to any decision based solely on automated Processing" that has legal or seriously impactful consequences, and require manual review (Art. 38(1), S-04). An exception covers decisions necessary for a contract (Art. 38(2)(a), S-04). The bank may rely on it only if the processing can be reviewed manually (Art. 38(5), S-04).
- AI-01 appears to be a "High Risk Processing Activities" case. The definition includes "a systematic and extensive evaluation of personal aspects relating to natural persons, based on automated Processing, including Profiling, and on which decisions are based that produce legal effects" (Schedule 1, S-04). Profiling means using automated processing to evaluate a person. AI-01 scores applicants and approves or declines loans (register).
- If so, the bank must carry out an impact assessment first (Art. 20(1), S-04). It must appoint a data protection officer if this is done on a systematic or regular basis (Art. 16(2)(b), S-04).
- If so, extra conditions also apply, including audit and certification requirements and an Autonomous Systems Officer, a role like a data protection officer (Reg. 10.3.3, S-03). The Commissioner is the DIFC's data protection regulator. The guidance says "no System may be used for High Risk Processing Activities until the Commissioner has promulgated these certification and other requirements" (Guidance on Reg. 10.3.3, S-03).
