# AI-04 angle B: the most practical reading of the EU AI Act
A working note, not an assessment. AI-04's tier stays "needs lawyer". The assumptions below are not known facts. Learning exercise, not legal advice.
## Assumptions (the most likely answer for a small bank)
- AI-04 ranks applicants only for jobs in Dubai. Nothing on file shows an EU office or EU hiring. So its output is not "used in the Union", and the Act does not reach it (Art. 2(1)(c), S-01). Everything below depends on this.
- The bank only uses AI-04. It is a deployer (user), not a provider (maker). It bought AI-04, and has not rebranded or substantially changed it (Arts. 3(4), 25(1)(a) and (b), S-01).
- Recruiters read only the top of the ranking, so lower-ranked applicants are rejected with no human review (Art. 38(1), S-04).
- The bank is incorporated in the DIFC (Art. 6(3), S-04). AI-04 is a semi-autonomous "System" (Reg. 10.1.1(a), S-03). The vendor runs it as "Operator", the party running it for the bank (Reg. 10.1.1(c), S-03).
- Applicants apply through a website, so the notice rule applies (Reg. 10.2.2, S-03).
- CV volumes are low, so this is not "extensive" evaluation or "High Risk Processing Activities" (Schedule 1, S-04). This is the weakest assumption.
- The GDPR (EU data protection law) does not apply, as the bank does not target applicants in the EU (Art. 3, S-05).
- Still unknown, as bank size does not answer them: whether the DIFC Commissioner has set certification rules (Reg. 10.3.3(a), S-03), and whether S-04 is the latest version.
## What the bank must do first, in order
1. Head of Human Resources confirms in writing where the rankings are used. This one fact decides scope (Art. 2(1)(c), S-01).
2. Have a person review applicants on request, as rejected applicants may object and require manual review (Art. 38(1), S-04).
3. Tell applicants about AI-04 clearly at first use (Reg. 10.2.2(a), S-03).
4. Record the lawful basis, meaning the legal reason for using the data, and the purpose (Arts. 9(1)(b) and (c), 10, S-04).
5. Get vendor evidence that AI-04 treats people "equally and fairly" (Reg. 10.3.1(b), S-03). Keep the risk and impact assessment ready to show on request (Reg. 10.2.2(d), S-03).
6. Stop AI-04 from deciding on sensitive data in CVs, such as health or religion (Arts. 11, 38(6), S-04).
7. Set up a way for applicants to complain and challenge the outcome (Reg. 10.3.5, S-03).
## If even one EU job is screened, the Act applies. Do these next
- Treat AI-04 as high-risk (Art. 6(2), S-01). Annex III, the list of high-risk uses, covers systems "to analyse and filter job applications, and to evaluate candidates" (Annex III, point 4(a), S-01). Profiling, meaning automated evaluation of a person, closes the exception (Arts. 3(52), 6(3), last subparagraph, S-01).
- Now: take measures to support staff AI literacy, meaning their understanding of AI (Art. 4, as replaced by Art. 1 point 5, S-02). The date is unconfirmed: this assumes Art. 4 falls under the 2 February 2025 rule for Chapters I and II (Art. 113, third paragraph, point (a), as replaced by Art. 1 point 40(a), S-02).
- By 2 December 2027 (Art. 113, third paragraph, point (c)(i), as replaced by Art. 1 point 40(b), S-02): follow the instructions (Art. 26(1), S-01), assign trained human oversight (Art. 26(2), S-01), monitor and report (Art. 26(5), S-01), and keep logs for at least six months (Art. 26(6), S-01).
- By the same date: tell applicants they are subject to AI-04 (Art. 26(11), S-01), and be ready to give "clear and meaningful explanations" (Art. 86(1), S-01).
- Possible grace rule: a system already in service is covered only if its design changes significantly from that date (Art. 111(2), as replaced by Art. 1 point 39(a), S-02). AI-04's service date is unknown. Do not rely on this without a lawyer.
## Left out on this reading
- Provider duties (Art. 16, S-01). AI notice by design, as applicants do not interact with AI-04 directly (Art. 50(1), S-01). Fundamental rights impact assessment (Art. 27(1), S-01). An Article 5 ban: no fact on file points to one (Art. 5, S-01).
