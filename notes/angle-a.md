# AI-04 angle A: the strictest reasonable reading of the EU AI Act
A working note, not an assessment. AI-04's tier stays "needs lawyer". The assumptions below are not known facts. Learning exercise, not legal advice.
## Assumptions (each one is the worst case for the bank)
- AI-04's rankings are used in the EU, so the Act applies to the bank (Art. 2(1)(c), S-01).
- AI-04 is high-risk (Art. 6(2), S-01). Annex III, the list of high-risk uses, covers systems "to analyse and filter job applications, and to evaluate candidates" (Annex III, point 4(a), S-01).
- AI-04 does profiling, meaning automated evaluation of a person. So the Art. 6(3) exception is closed, as such a system "shall always be considered to be high-risk" (Art. 6(3), last subparagraph, and Art. 3(52), S-01).
- The bank is a deployer (user) (Art. 3(4), S-01). It is also a provider (maker), because it put its own name on AI-04 or changed it substantially (Art. 25(1)(a) and (b), S-01). The register says "bought", so this is the most stretched assumption.
- Applicants use AI-04 directly. No relief for financial institutions applies (Art. 26(5) and (6), S-01). AI-04 gets no grace period (Art. 111(2), as replaced by Art. 1 point 39(a), S-02).
## Duties as deployer
- Use AI-04 according to its instructions (Art. 26(1), S-01).
- Give human oversight to people with the competence, training and authority for it (Art. 26(2), S-01).
- Keep input data relevant and representative, where the bank controls it (Art. 26(4), S-01).
- Monitor AI-04. Report risks and serious incidents, and suspend use if it presents a risk (Art. 26(5), S-01).
- Keep its automatic logs (records of what it did) for at least six months (Art. 26(6), S-01).
- Tell staff and their representatives before workplace use, reading hiring as workplace use (Art. 26(7), S-01).
- Use the provider's information for a data protection impact assessment (Art. 26(9), S-01, and Art. 35, S-05).
- Tell applicants that they are subject to AI-04 (Art. 26(11), S-01).
- Give affected applicants "clear and meaningful explanations" of AI-04's role in the decision (Art. 86(1), S-01).
- Cooperate with the authorities (Art. 26(12), S-01).
- Support the AI literacy of staff, meaning their understanding of AI (Art. 4, as replaced by Art. 1 point 5, S-02).
## Duties as provider (these apply on top of the deployer duties)
- Build in risk management, data governance, technical documents, record-keeping, information for deployers, human oversight, and accuracy, robustness and cybersecurity (Arts. 9 to 15, S-01; Arts. 10 and 11 as amended by Art. 1 points 9 and 10, S-02).
- Have a quality management system, kept documents and logs, a conformity assessment (a check against the rules), an EU declaration, the CE mark (EU conformity mark) and registration (Art. 16(c) to (i), S-01).
- Appoint an authorised representative, an agent based in the EU (Art. 22(1), S-01). This assumes in-house use counts.
- Design AI-04 so that applicants know they are dealing with AI, if it interacts with them directly (Art. 50(1), S-01).
## Deadlines
- High-risk duties: 2 December 2027 (Art. 113, third paragraph, point (c)(i), as replaced by Art. 1 point 40(b), S-02). Art. 4 duty: already applies (Art. 113, third paragraph, point (a), as replaced by Art. 1 point 40(a), S-02). Art. 50: 2 August 2026, already passed (Art. 113, second paragraph, S-01). Art. 86: unknown whether it bites before December 2027.
## Left out, even on this reading
- Fundamental rights impact assessment: it covers public-law bodies, private entities providing public services, and Annex III points 5(b) and (c), not point 4 (Art. 27(1), S-01). An Article 5 ban: no fact on file points to one (Art. 5, S-01).
